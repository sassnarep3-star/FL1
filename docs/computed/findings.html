2. What the control proves

Betting every home team at the close, 5,067 settled bets, returned -5.2% with a hit rate of 48.6% against a breakeven of 105%. That gap is the vig. Any strategy that lands within about ±3% of zero is doing nothing but paying it. This control is the reason the leaderboard reports rejections rather than ranking noise.

4. Availability is measurable; depth is not

The official Week 10 2026 report was read row by row: 345 player rows across 28 clubs, 66 Out, 10 Doubtful, 84 Questionable, each with the official URL and a verbatim quote. The injury picture joins cleanly to the 14 games. What does not exist is a depth order: the official roster feed lists status and jersey number, not a sequence (FLAG-02). So the system reports QB changes by start sequence and refuses to call anyone a “3rd string” without a source. See Injuries & depth.

5. Data access is the binding constraint

The single highest-value fix is not a model, it is an allowlist entry (FLAG-01). Five of six planned datasets were unreachable, which is why the availability history is one season and there are no props or snap counts. Every downstream gap on this site traces back to that one item.

6. What would change the answer

Not a better formula. Three things, in order: an opening-line feed (so an edge can be measured against a price we can actually get), a depth-order feed with timestamps (so the requirement can be met at all), and a signed gate config (so “skill” stops being a matter of opinion). All three are in Suggestions as SG-01 to SG-05.
