Leaderboard competition design

Design for the rewarded internal competition the brief asks for. The central design choice is that the leaderboard ranks a luck-adjusted statistic computed from a sealed, walk-forward bet log — not profit, not ROI, not this month's hot hand — and that rewards are conditional on gate passage. engine/leaderboard.py implements exactly the scoring written here, and its unit tests are hand-checkable.

RuleDefeats
Sealed entries: hash(code + config + data snapshot) submitted before the season starts; ranks computed only from the sealed version.Retro-fitting the strategy to what happened, and the quiet edit after a bad week.

Overlap test (Jaccard > 0.8 merges entries); max 5 live entries per author.Entering the same idea twenty times and keeping the winner — the winner's-curse reintroduced at the contest level (G-13).
Settlement only from the pre-declared official source; the operator's own settlement is used for void/reprice effects and nothing else.Stat-definition arbitrage and 'we would have won on a different definition' disputes (Q-03).
Price at bet time is captured from the posted line at that second, with the timestamp stored.Claiming a better price than was available, which fabricates CLV — the very statistic the ranking is built on.
No use of non-public information; inputs restricted to the source register by build-time check.The one 'edge' that would end the programme and possibly more: leaked availability information (Q-23, GAP-11).
Stake caps are part of the rules, not advice: an entry that exceeds the exposure caps is disqualified, not penalised.Winning by taking a ruinous amount of risk on a positive-variance bet.
Independent validator: a second person (or session) re-runs the winning entry from the sealed artefacts before a reward is paid.Everything the builder of a model cannot see about their own model (S-19).
