Injury, availability and depth-deficiency tracking

The mechanism by which the system 'knows deficiencies in defences and offences' — built only on what the league publishes, with every inference labelled as an inference. Two principles govern this file: (1) the report tells us who is unavailable, not how good the replacement is, so the quality gap is ours to estimate and must be flagged as such; (2) absence and snap share are different variables — the second is what actually moves a number.

B. Next-Man-Up (NMU) deficiency index — specification

One number per unit-group per club per game, expressed in points-per-game-equivalent so it can be summed into the scoring model instead of being a vague 'injury burden' count. Deliberately built as a difference of expectations, because 'starter out' without a replacement quality gap is not a model input, it is a headline.

B. Next-Man-Up (NMU) deficiency index — specification
KeyValue
Formuladeficiency(group) = sum over absent players of snap_share(player) x [ Q(player, opponent) - Q(expected replacement, opponent) ] where Q is a per-position contribution estimate in points. Group-level output is clipped at a floor so a 4th-string WR absence does not masquerade as a starting-quality hit.
Snap shareEstimated from our own play logs (GAP-04/GAP-05). Until those exist, snap_share defaults to 1.0 for a listed starter and 0.0 otherwise, and every downstream number is printed with the tag 'snap-share unmodelled'.
Q() estimatorLeft blank. It must be fitted from data we hold with an out-of-sample log-loss comparison against a league-average-replacement baseline; if the fitted model cannot beat 'assume league-average replacement', we use the naive version and publish that we could not do better. No hand-set position weights.
Two-sided symmetryThe brief asks for defensive deficiency (backups replacing starters) and the offensive equivalent. Both use the same index with different groups: defence = edge CB, slot CB, OLB, ILB, DL interior, safety shell; offence = QB regime, O-line (tackle vs interior), TE, RB committee depth, WR depth. The QB case is a separate discrete regime, not another term (models.json S6).
Coverage-shell interactionThe index is additive by construction; the verified Week 10 example of a starting outside corner and the deep-middle safety both Out for the same club is precisely the kind of interaction that additive terms understate. An interaction term is specified but gated: it may only enter if it improves out-of-sample log-loss over the additive form (SIG-05).
Availability scenariosRather than a single expectation, we hold three scenarios per game — best case (all Questionable play), expected (per fitted P(play)), worst case (all Questionable sit) — and price a line as the scenario-weighted mixture. The spread is reported with its scenario range, because on a week where six starters are Questionable the point estimate is a fiction.
Change detectionThe tradeable object is the diff between consecutive official captures, keyed by (game, club, player, day), including the transition to/from 'not listed' (which per policy means certain to play). Every capture is stored raw with a timestamp so a diff can be audited after the fact.
Anti-patterns we forbid in code reviewhard-coded designation probabilities; treating a blank injury cell as 'not injured'; counting rest DNPs as injuries; using a player's absence to justify a bet whose subject is that player's health (prohibited class); assuming the depth-chart order is the usage order.

D. Prohibited by design
RuleBasis
We do not bet on injuries, and we do not build a market on whether a player plays.The league's published prohibited classes include markets whose subject is player injuries, and pre-determined outcomes such as 'Will QB start this week?' — the league asks its partners not to offer them.
We do not accept, solicit, or use non-public availability information, from any source, however good the signal.The league's gambling policy prohibits sharing inside information such as injuries or lineup changes, monitors betting patterns for indications of a leak, and assigns an integrity representative to every club. Being on the wrong side of that is not a risk premium.
Injury data may only be used as an input to a permitted market (spread, total, team total, permitted stat props).The distinction is between what we know and what we bet on; the league restricts the latter.
No prop priced on a single person's one-play decision.Prohibited class: outcomes 100% determinable by one person in one play (e.g. a kicker to miss, a QB's first pass to be incomplete).

Verification rule. Every factual row links to an official source — the league (nfl.com and its subdomains), a state gaming regulator, or a peer-reviewed publication.
