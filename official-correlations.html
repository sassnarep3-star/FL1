Official-source correlations

Correlations computed only from officially sourced numbers, and every value published with its n. Nothing from a third-party feed enters this table.

Policy. The inputs are the verified results feed (results-feed.html) — 137 games whose finals are row-level official (nfl.com week pages, S49–S58). The measurement register (data/correlations.json, C-01–C-15) stays blank under the same rule until each pair’s inputs are officially sourced (flag F-11, open). Player-prop correlations (Prop Correlations) are computed from a third-party archive and are published separately with that label (flag F17) — they are not mixed in here.

What the numbers say (and do not say)

  • Same-game scoring co-movement (C-O1, r=+0.076, n=137) and week-to-week form persistence (C-O2/C-O3, |r|<0.09, n=242) are all near zero in this sample. On officially sourced numbers alone, a bettor assuming hot-hand scoring persistence is leaning on noise — and same-game parlay legs priced as if independent are close to the truth at the game total level (player-level dependence is a different question — see the third-party prop table).
  • These are small-sample values (one season’s first ten weeks). n is published precisely so the reader can discount them; the feed grows weekly and the script recomputes deterministically.
  • Correlation is not causation and none of these edges a market: they calibrate assumptions (parlay leg dependence, form reversion) that the origination and pricing layers consume.
