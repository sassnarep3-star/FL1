Validation Gates

Eight gates. A strategy must pass every one that applies to it before it can be ranked. The gates are defined here, before any strategy exists, precisely so they cannot be rewritten to fit a result.

Removing luck: three separate tests, never one blended score

The brief asks to remove luck from the equation. That is only meaningful if it is operational, so it is defined as three tests that must each hold. They are reported side by side and are never averaged, because averaging lets a strong result on one hide a failure on another.

Correlation

A parlay's price depends on the joint probability of its legs. If two legs are positively correlated, multiplying their individual probabilities understates the joint probability and misstates the edge. The reverse is true for negatively correlated legs.

Correlation is therefore estimated from repeated joint outcomes of the same pairing, and never assumed. A pairing needs many observations before an estimate means anything; below that threshold the value stays blank.

Acceptance thresholds

The numeric thresholds are deliberately not set on this page. Setting them after seeing a result is the single easiest way to manufacture apparent skill, and choosing them before a benchmark price exists would be arbitrary. They are registered as open question Q09 and must be fixed in one commit before any grading begins.

G-14 — the demotion gate (requirement R28): live strategies return to paper

A gate set decides who gets promoted. This gate decides who gets sent back. Without it, a live badge outlives the edge that earned it and the leaderboard decays into a graveyard of former winners. Any ONE trigger demotes; the verdict logic is not reviewable, the thresholds are.

States: LIVE → (any trigger) → PAPER; PAPER → LIVE only through re-promotion on a fresh sealed sample. Old bets never re-qualify an entry — the burden of proof resets.
