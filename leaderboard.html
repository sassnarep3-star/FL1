The leaderboard this repository wants

Most model leaderboards rank strategies by win rate or by season-long return. Both are noise. Ten bets can produce a 90% hit rate from a coin, and a season's return can be one lucky Sunday. This page ranks by a proper score instead: a score that a forecast cannot improve by lying, measured against the market's own de-vigged probability, with an uncertainty interval and a multiplicity correction. Then it shows the whole table going the other way, because that is what the data says.

The result, stated plainly

No strategy in this corpus has demonstrated skill

Every strategy's proper-score skill against the de-vigged market is negative, and every game-cluster lower bound is below zero. The best entry is the least negative, not a winner. Two things follow, and both are published rather than buried:

1. A negative skill here means the model's line is a worse probability forecast than the price it was betting against. The model's lines are more accurate than the market's lines in points — see the origination page — but a line is not a probability, and when both are converted to probabilities with the same residual scale, the market's price wins.

2. The strategy that looks best by return is not the strategy that looks best by proper score, and neither qualifies. The staking ledger is published separately, on its own page, and is never merged into this ranking.

The two-stage rule therefore ends at stage one for every entry: none is promoted to paper, and none is on the live leaderboard. The demo division exists to be honest, not to be populated.

The four divisions, and their gates

Singles are ranked. Every combo division is gated, because a combo priced by multiplying marginal probabilities is invalid: the legs are dependent. A combo can only enter when a directly validated joint probability exists AND the operator's void/push rules are documented. Neither exists in this repository, so the gate is closed and the reason is published.

How the numbers are made

The score

Every settled trade is a probability forecast of a binary event. The Brier score is the mean squared distance between the forecast and the outcome. It is a proper scoring rule: the forecast that minimises it is the true probability, so a forecast cannot do better by being less honest. Skill is measured against the market:

BSS = 1 - Brier(model) / Brier(market)

Zero means “no sharper than the price”; a positive number means sharper. The market's probability is taken from both posted prices with the vig removed, because comparing a vig-free model probability against a vig-inflated price would flatter the model for a reason that has nothing to do with skill.

Turning a line into a probability

A line is not a probability. To compare a line against a price, the line has to be converted. The conversion uses one shared residual scale — the standard deviation of the market line's own error, measured over every settled bet (12.68 points for spreads, 13.21 for totals). Using one scale for both sides is deliberate: it isolates the comparison onto the line, which is what is being originated, and stops a flatter forecast from winning on calibration alone. Line accuracy in points is published on its own column, so a reader who cares about the line can read the line.

Uncertainty that respects the game

Bets inside one game are not independent: a model that is wrong about one club is usually wrong about its opponent, and one bad weather read hits both. The interval is therefore a cluster bootstrap that resamples games, not bets. The published bound is the 5th percentile — the pessimistic end of 2,000 draws.

Multiplicity

If many strategies are ranked on the same data, the best is partly luck. The one-sided p-value that each strategy's skill exceeds zero is adjusted across all strategies with the Holm step-down procedure, so a strategy cannot win by being the luckiest of many.

The controls

Random picks draw the same number of bets from the same games at random. Favourites only always takes the side the market priced shorter. A strategy that cannot beat both has demonstrated a run, not skill. Both are scored with the same metric, so the comparison is like for like.

The split

Seasons are assigned to train, validation and a final holdout before any number is computed, and the assignment is committed at data/splits.json. The holdout is counted here so its size is on the record, but no published number on this site is computed from it, and the rule for reading it is written down rather than remembered.

The two stages

Stage one is the proper score: cluster lower bound above zero, Holm-adjusted p below 0.05, and both controls beaten. Stage two is a prospective CLV/ROI escrow that only accepts bets made after the pre-registration commit. The backtest corpus contains no prospective bets, so no strategy can reach stage two from it. That is the point of the escrow: it cannot be satisfied by data that already existed when the rule was written.
