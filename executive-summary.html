Executive Summary

Status as of 2026-11-14. This page states only what can be traced to an official source, and marks everything else as open.

1. What has been verified

The repository was empty apart from a five-byte README, so the entire base is new. Fifteen sources were attempted; 73 loaded successfully and 8 did not. The failed ones are recorded as unverified and are never used to support a claim.

The most substantial verified artefact is the official Week 10, 2026 season injury report: 345 player rows over 28 clubs, with each player's practice status and game status reproduced exactly as published. Of those rows, 160 carry a game status (Out, Doubtful or Questionable) and 94 are recorded as did-not-participate in practice. Two rows are explicitly not injury related, which matters: counting them as injuries would inflate any injury rate.

Alongside the injury data the site holds the official 2026 division and league standings for all 32 clubs, the Week 10 schedule with kickoff times and broadcasters, the league's tiebreaking procedures, and the official league calendar through Super Bowl LXI on 2027-02-14.

2. What is blocked, and why

Three blockers stop the analytical work, and none of them can be solved by effort inside this repository.

  1. No benchmark price. The league publishes results, statistics and schedules, not betting prices. Without a price there is no edge, no expected value and no closing-line value. Every such field on this site is blank. This is blocker #1 and it gates the entire leaderboard.
  2. No official snap counts and no league-wide official depth chart. nfl.com/depth-charts/ returns 404. Depth therefore has to be captured club by club, and each club titles its own chart Unofficial Depth Chart. 31 of the 32 clubs publish one as a readable table; Houston publishes its as an image (F16). So “which backup replaced which starter” cannot be measured, only alleged. The site shows official absence and the club’s own published ordering instead, and labels the depth layer as derived.
  3. One snapshot, one season. Everything here is a single capture on a single day. No backtest, no persistence claim and no multi-season holdout is possible from this data.

3. What the injury data already shows

Among the clubs captured, 66 players are designated Out, split 37 on offense, 27 on defense and 2 on special teams. Defensive line and secondary depth is the most exposed area, which is the part of the brief about 2nd and 3rd string defenders being forced in.

The pattern is concrete and checkable across the league: of the 753 first-string slots charted, 71 carry an official Out / Doubtful / Questionable status this week, and for each one the club’s own chart names the next player at that same slot. Kansas City is worked end to end on Backup Depth — starting right tackle Out (ankle), starting running back Questionable after not practising (groin), second-string defensive tackle Out (neck). Those are observed facts with a source link. The claim that a specific backup took the snaps is not made, because no snap-count source was verified.

4. The five decisions that unblock the rest

  1. Name one market to price first (Q01). Trying to price everything at once guarantees nothing reaches significance in an 18-week season.
  2. Supply or accept a benchmark price source (Q03), or accept that the site publishes fair value only and grades on calibration instead of profit.
  3. Accept that depth is derived, not official (Q04), and let the depth layer ship labelled as such.
  4. Pre-register the validation gates (Q09, Q12, Q19) before any strategy is scored, so the target cannot be moved after the result is known.
  5. Keep rewards non-monetary (Q13, Q14) and leave the legal question open rather than answering it.

5. Recommendations, in order

Full reasoning and alternatives are on the Suggestions page. In short:

  • Publish fair value with an uncertainty band, never an invented market price (SG01).
  • Rank strategies on calibration first, profit second (SG02).
  • Seal every prediction with a hash before kickoff (SG03) — it is almost free and it is what makes a record credible.
  • Split the injury product into observed absence and derived depth, visibly (SG04, SG12).
  • Require every strategy to beat a published naive baseline, or it does not get ranked (SG08).
  • Sequence the work: team-level single market, then props, then combos, then correlations (SG07).

6. What this site deliberately does not do

It does not quote an injury-policy definition, because the official PDF returns 404. It does not claim any club has a bye, because no official bye table was verified. It does not resolve two places where official NFL.com pages contradict each other. It does not print a single performance number. Each of those is listed with links on Flagged for Review.
