Line origination

Three markets per game, produced in the order the brief demands: the side first, then the total, then the moneyline. Each row publishes our fair number, the market number, and the difference between them as three separate columns — never a blended figure, never a number chosen to make the other two agree.

The order is enforced in code, not by convention. OriginationPipeline.originate_total() and derive_moneyline() raise StageOrderError unless originate_side() has run for that game. That is not decoration: the moneyline is a re-statement of the margin distribution, so fitting it separately to prices would create three models that cannot be reconciled. A unit test asserts the raise.

B. What is deliberately not estimated

  • market prices (read from the price store, never estimated)
  • injury designation probabilities (no official mapping exists; see engine/injuries.py)
  • replacement-quality to points conversion (no snap counts; see flagged items)

The third bullet is the one a reader most often expects to find filled in: this site publishes how deep a replacement sits, not what it is worth in points, because the conversion needs snap counts and no official source publishes them (depth carries the same rule).

The two MAE columns side by side are the whole point: our distance from the market, and our distance from what actually happened, next to the market's own distance from what happened. Where ours is larger than the market's, the model is worse than the number it is arguing with, and that is published rather than smoothed.

E. Reproduce it

python3 scripts/build_market_spine.py regenerates every number on this page from the captures in data/raw/; python3 scripts/build_market_spine.py --check proves the committed artefacts are byte-identical to a fresh run. The pipeline is engine/originate_spine.py, the settlement it is graded against is engine/results_ledger.py, and the price rows it quotes are in the store.
